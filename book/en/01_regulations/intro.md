# TFG Regulations

The first practical decision when starting the documentation for the Final Degree Project (TFG) in Computer Engineering is to review the regulations that govern the subject. In the Computer Engineering degree there are three levels that should be kept separate:

- The general TFG regulations of the University of Salamanca.
- The regulations and procedures of the Faculty of Sciences.
- The specific page of the Department of Computer Science and Automation for the Computer Engineering degree.

In addition, the technical documentation recommended by the Department is related to professional standards for Computer Engineering projects. This relationship does not turn your TFG into a complete formally certified professional project, but it does explain the origin of this documentation and why the report, technical annexes, traceability, planning, and justification of decisions are required.

```{tip}
Always keep the official links up to date and record the version or academic year consulted. Web pages change; your report must be able to justify which documentation you followed.
```

## Course Description

The academic course description in USAL Guides is another official source that should be located and reviewed in its current version. The description consulted identifies the Final Degree Project for the Computer Engineering degree with code `101147`, `12 ECTS`, Final Degree Project status, and placement in the 4th year {cite:p}`usal_guia_tfg_informatica_2025_2026`.

This description does not replace the regulations or the instructions issued by the Faculty and the Department. Its value lies in defining the academic nature of the subject: it is not an extended practical assignment or a simple software demo, but a final subject with a report, supervision through tutorials (with assigned hours), and a public defense before an evaluation panel.

## What Is a TFG in Computer Engineering?

In the Computer Engineering degree, the TFG is an **integrative** piece of work: it must bring together degree competences and apply them to a project or piece of work related to the contents of the degree. The official course description presents it as a synthesis exercise in which **the student learns to develop, document, present, and properly defend their work before a specialized audience** {cite:p}`usal_guia_tfg_informatica_2025_2026`.

In practical terms, this implies three responsibilities:

- **Build something with sufficient technical substance**: software, a prototype, applied research, analysis, integration, a tool, a system, or a study related to computer science.
- **Document it** so that another person can understand the problem, the decisions, the solution, the tests, the limitations, and future work.
- **Defend it orally** with technical rigor, clarity, and command of the subject.

That is why this book insists that documentation is not later bureaucracy. It is part of the TFG itself: it records evidence of how you worked and allows the panel to evaluate not only the final result, but also the engineering judgment behind the decisions made. In an academic world with AI, it is more important than ever to show that the work is yours, that you understand what you have done, and that you can explain it to others.
