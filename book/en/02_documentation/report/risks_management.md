# Risks and management

This block should show that the project was managed with judgment. The DIA v2 guide distinguishes risk analysis, organization and project management {cite:p}`dia_documentacion_tfg_v2`. The report should not include a decorative Gantt chart; it should explain how work was organized and how problems were handled.

## Risk analysis

Select risks that truly affect the TFG:

- **Acceptance**: users do not understand, need or adopt the solution.
- **Technology**: a library, API, model, device or architecture does not behave as expected.
- **Data**: lack of data, bias, poor quality, privacy or permissions.
- **Integration**: problems connecting the solution to a larger system.
- **Planning**: underestimated tasks, external dependencies or scope changes.

You may use a risk matrix, SWOT analysis or risk register. A minimal register can include number, description, priority, complexity and corrective measures. What matters is that each risk has a response: accept, reduce, avoid, transfer or monitor.

## Work organization

Explain the real phases of the project and how the chosen methodology was applied. The path from proposal to final solution should be visible:

- initial analysis;
- design;
- implementation;
- testing;
- documentation review;
- defense preparation.

When architecture, components, protocols, entities, databases or algorithms are important, summarize the organization here and refer to the analysis and design annex for detail.

## Project management

Include resources, timeline, milestones and deviations. If changes occurred, explain them naturally: a serious TFG is not one without problems, but one that identifies, manages and justifies adjustments.

The Gantt chart, if included, should tell the real story of the work. Connect planning, risks, decisions and final scope.
