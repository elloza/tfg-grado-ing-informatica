# Introduction

The introduction opens the report and should orient the committee before entering technical detail. According to the DIA v2 guide, it should present the problem domain, its context, possible solutions, the motivation for the work and the overall structure of the documentation {cite:p}`dia_documentacion_tfg_v2`.

A useful introduction answers these questions early:

- What domain does the TFG address?
- What problem, need or opportunity has been identified?
- Why does it make sense as a Computer Engineering TFG?
- What general approach has been followed?
- How is the report organized?

Do not duplicate the abstract. The abstract condenses the whole work; the introduction prepares the reader. It may mention general alternatives, but it should not reveal the whole design or become a tutorial on the technologies used.

## Recommended structure

A solid structure is:

1. Present the context and application area.
2. Explain the problem with concrete examples.
3. Justify the academic, technical or social motivation.
4. Anticipate the type of solution or study performed.
5. Close with a short map of the document.

Keep the wording precise. Avoid generic statements if they do not support the project. It is better to explain what real limitation exists, who it affects and why your work provides an assessable response.
