# Conclusions

The conclusions close the report by returning to the objectives. The DIA v2 guide asks the author to make clear whether the objectives were achieved, what problems appeared, how they were solved, what technical and personal learning took place, what limitations remain and what future lines of work are possible {cite:p}`dia_documentacion_tfg_v2`.

A serious conclusion does not merely state that the objectives were met. It should show it:

- objective;
- result obtained;
- evidence used to verify it;
- limitation found;
- possible improvement.

## Results and learning

Summarize the main results without repeating the whole solution. Explain what worked, what did not go as far as expected and which decisions mattered most.

It is also reasonable to include professional and personal learning, as long as it is connected to the TFG: technologies learned, design judgment, time management, communication with users, data handling, documentation, testing or deployment.

## Limitations and future work

Limitations are not failures; they are the honest boundary of the work. They may relate to time, data, performance, scale, validation, user access, privacy, generalization or maintenance.

Future work should be realistic and connected to what was actually done:

- extending a delimited functionality;
- improving performance or accessibility;
- validating with more users or data;
- integrating with another system;
- strengthening security or deployment;
- turning a prototype into a maintainable product.

## Bibliography before submission

The DIA v2 guide recommends a standard citation format and mentions APA as a practical option {cite:p}`dia_documentacion_tfg_v2`. This book uses an APA-like author-year style. Before submission, check three things: every in-text citation appears in the bibliography, every bibliography entry has been cited at least once, and the sources are relevant to the TFG topic.
